# Spectral Emission + Distance-Kernel Photon GI — Direction (2026-07)

**Status:** DIRECTION / exploratory — NOT scheduled, NOT verified. Captured from a design conversation 2026-07-10 (user-driven). Downstream of the Sampled-Lighting program's *optional/future photon layer* (`Sampled-Lighting-Design-2026-07.md` §2, §4 "Later"), NOT on the near-term ReSTIR+DDGI spine. Needs a research pass before any increment.

**One line:** emission is a voxel channel that averages up the mip pyramid (bounded emitter count), its spectrum is derived from temperature (blackbody → natural star/heat/ember color), and a photon layer carries spectral values reconstructed by a **distance-modulated (amplitude + phase) gather kernel** — a phenomenological, tune-for-look wave approximation, not coherent optics.

---

## 1. Three connected ideas (in dependency order)

### 1a. Emission as a mip-averaged voxel channel → a light-tree for free
- Emission is another semantic channel in the SoA voxel format (Inc3 multi-channel contract: sdf+color+roughness+**emission**). A voxel with emission>0 shades itself (add emitted radiance to the pixel unconditionally) — self-glow is trivial and near-free.
- **Key insight:** emission averages UP the sparse-mip ESVO like color/occupancy. A million glowing voxels become a handful of bright emissive nodes at a coarse mip. So "emissive-as-light" is NOT a million-area-light problem — it's a **bounded cut through the emissive mip tree** at a LOD chosen by solid-angle/distance (distant glowing cluster = one bright coarse voxel; nearby = resolves to fine voxels).
- This is the classic many-light hierarchy / light-tree (Walter Lightcuts; ReSTIR light-BVH) — except the emission mip pyramid GIVES it to us for free, reusing the LOD structure already built. Composes with ReSTIR (reservoir samples nodes of the cut) and DDGI (probes gather from whatever mip the ray hits).
- **This sub-idea is the strongest and most independent** — it sharpens Inc3/Inc4 emissive handling even WITHOUT the spectral/photon parts. Adopt-able on its own.

### 1b. Temperature → spectral emission (blackbody), spectral↔RGB converter
- Store frequency+amplitude (a SPECTRUM), not RGB, for emission. Derive it from a voxel's TEMPERATURE via Planck's law (blackbody): a star's color falls out of its temperature; a cooling ember shifts red→black; heating metal white→orange — physically-correct-enough color with zero art-authoring.
- Unifies with the physics/delta-baseline program: heat *circulation* (temperature field evolving) drives emission color automatically. One physical quantity (temperature) → emission spectrum → perceived color.
- Needs a spectral→RGB converter at the final gather (CIE / blackbody-locus map — the achievable, well-trodden part of spectral rendering).
- Correct for: blackbody emission (stars/heat/fire/embers), and — if extended to transport — dispersion, iridescence, fluorescence (RGB fakes these badly). But for THIS direction the target is emission color, not full spectral transport.

### 1c. Distance-kernel spectral-photon gather (the "good-enough wave")
- Photons carry (frequency, phase, amplitude) — spectral, from 1b, count-bounded by 1a's mips.
- **The move:** at gather time, weight each photon by the sampling ray's PERPENDICULAR MISS DISTANCE — modulate amplitude AND phase by that distance. Summing many distance-phase-modulated photon contributions in a neighborhood APPROXIMATES a wavefront (fringe/banding/chromatic-falloff character) WITHOUT propagating a real wave.
- This is photon mapping's existing gather kernel (already weights photons by distance within a radius — cone/Gaussian) made **spectral + phase-aware**. A small, LOCAL, per-gather change to machinery photon mapping already has. No global coherent transport, no path-vs-path interference — the "interference" is just the emergent result of summing distance-phase-modulated neighbors.

## 2. Explicit boundaries (considered decisions, not gaps)
- **Phenomenological, NOT physics.** Deliberately trades accuracy for a convincing look — that is what makes it tractable rather than a coherent-optics rabbit hole. Its gate is VISUAL ("does it read as wave-like / does the star look right"), NOT a numeric mirror-test (unlike BRDF/VNDF, which validate against equations). Nobody should later "correct" this toward real optics — that kills the cheapness for no target benefit.
- **Phase is LOCAL-ONLY.** Phase is a per-photon parameter consumed ONLY inside the gather kernel (distance modulation), NEVER propagated coherently across the scene. Global coherent wave transport (interference/diffraction across all paths) is holography-grade, wildly beyond real-time, and unnecessary for incoherent sources (sun/fire/stars don't interfere) — explicitly OUT OF SCOPE. The local-gather use is the one framing where carrying phase is cheap and buys character.
- **Rides the OPTIONAL photon layer, not the spine.** This is a spectral-photon variant of the parked SPPM layer (`Sampled-Lighting-Design` §4 "Later"). It does NOT fork the near-term ReSTIR(Inc3)+DDGI(Inc4) program — it's exploratory, can be bold without destabilizing Inc1-4.
- **World-space voxel emitter sampling is at the EDGE of verified research** (pass-2 caveat: ReSTIR numbers assume triangle emitters + screen-space motion vectors; per-brick/per-mip emitter reservoirs are "world-space ReSTIR" territory needing its own research pass). 1a's mip-cut mitigates the count problem but the sampling/importance detail is unverified.

## 2b. Efficiency model — "the LOD/traversal structures ARE the lighting-physics accelerators"

Organizing thesis (from the 2026-07-10 design conversation): every structure already built for LOD and traversal simultaneously answers a lighting-physics question, so the spectral/photon model is NOT a separate expensive system bolted on — it reuses hierarchy the renderer already holds. The efficiency principle throughout: **spectra live ONLY in the emit→transport→cache leg; the whole scene never carries spectra per-pixel; collapse spectral→RGB ONCE at the probe cell / gather.**

- **Probe-cached photon accumulation + short temporal memory (the budget-maker).** Photon *throughput* is what got real-time photon GI refuted (pass 1). Fix: photons deposit into a WORLD-SPACE PROBE CACHE that accumulates over a short temporal window (EWMA/bounded history), so each frame contributes only a FRACTION of the photons and the probe integrates them over time. Diffuse indirect is low-frequency + temporally stable → nearly lossless perceptually ("GI consistency"). **This IS the DDGI mechanism doing double duty** — probes already accumulate radiance with hysteresis; here the update SOURCE is photon deposition instead of ray-gathered radiance. Unifies the photon and DDGI directions into ONE cached-GI structure, not two. Declared bias = lag (moving emitter's GI fades in over the hysteresis window — same class DDGI already carries); diffuse-indirect only, sharp direct shadows stay per-pixel. **This is the mechanism that could make a photon backbone actually hit budget.**
- **Convert spectral→RGB per-cell, ONCE, not per-photon-sample.** N photons accumulate into a probe cell → convert the AGGREGATE once, not each photon then sum RGB. N× saving AND more correct (summing spectra then converting ≠ converting then summing, once the CIE integral's nonlinearity is involved). **Correction of framing:** spectral→RGB is NOT an FFT — it is an integral against the three CIE color-matching curves (three weighted dot-products ∫spectrum·x̄,·ȳ,·z̄). An FFT is the right tool ONLY for wave(amplitude+phase)→spectrum, which sits BEFORE the CIE step: `wave --FFT--> spectrum --CIE--> RGB`. **OPEN CHOICE (determines whether FFT is needed at all):** do probe cells store accumulated SPECTRA (each photon adds power at its wavelength → skip straight to CIE, no FFT, wave-character comes from the distance-gather-kernel) or accumulated WAVEFORMS (amplitude+phase summed to preserve distance-phase-modulation within a cell → then one FFT + one CIE per cell, where "one transformation into RGB instead of per-sample" is literally true)? Both viable; waveform-per-cell is the more ambitious version.
- **Redshift/blueshift = per-emitter wavelength SHIFT (a shift, not a filter).** Doppler/cosmological shift is λ→λ·(1+z): in a spectrum, slide it along the wavelength axis, ONE scalar per emitter, applied once at emit/deposit — NOT per pixel. Impossible-to-do-right in RGB (the spectrum is already collapsed; a hue-rotate is wrong for a blackbody — a redshifted blue-white star reddens THROUGH white as its whole Planck curve shifts). **Parametric shortcut:** a redshifted BLACKBODY is literally just SCALING THE TEMPERATURE (Wien) — a single float, stays an exact Planck curve. Poster child for "spectral is the only correct path, and it's nearly free here."
- **Redshift-by-mip-level (distance for free).** The mip level a sample lands on already encodes distance-scale (solid-angle LOD → a sample 17 levels up is ~2^17 units ± half-boundary). So the cosmological-redshift approximation (shift ∝ distance) becomes `shiftFactor(mipLevel)` — a lookup by the level you were ALREADY going to sample, zero new distance math. The effect and the LOD share the same axis (both grow with distance) — this isn't a bolt-on, it's recognizing the quantity you need already selected your sample. Composes with the parametric path: distant star's color = sample coarse emissive mip node → its temperature → ×redshift(mipLevel) → CIE once. Ties lighting directly to the tiered-ESVO scale hierarchy ([[tiered-esvo-observer-address-direction]]). BOUNDARY: it's the sample's OWN distance-scale, quantized to powers of two ± half-boundary, NOT true integrated path length through intervening tiers — fine for LOOK (visual gate), NOT for a real cosmological-distance simulation (which would need accumulated path length). Don't "correct" it toward rigor — that throws away the free-ness.
- **Solidity is RESOLUTION-DEPENDENT: fill-fraction-as-opacity at coarse mips (the "distant solid looks volumetric" pillar).** "Occupied" at a fine leaf = solid/opaque; the SAME region averaged up to a coarse mip = "contains some stuff, mostly vacuum" → translucent. Saturn's rings (trillions of opaque rocks → translucent sheet), comet belts, clouds, galaxies (point stars in vacuum → luminous fog) are all this: from far away we don't see anything concrete, we see an ACCUMULATION across large distance. **Mechanism, free from the sparse-mip ESVO:** the mip pyramid already averages OCCUPANCY up the levels — that average FILL FRACTION *is* the coverage/opacity (a 3%-occupied coarse node → α≈0.03 per unit traversed). Rule: at coarse mips, a hit is NOT an opaque stop — accumulate `α = fillFraction` and keep marching front-to-back (alpha composite) until opacity saturates. Near → resolves to fine voxels, solid as ever; far → integrates coverage across the coarse node, translucent. Transition is CONTINUOUS + automatic with the solid-angle-chosen mip, same ray, same structure. This is voxel-cone-tracing's pre-integrated-opacity mechanic (pass-2), but reframed: VCT's "light leaks through averaged opacity" BUG becomes the intended FEATURE — averaged opacity is exactly correct when the thing genuinely IS mostly-empty-at-that-scale. **Completes the thesis:** at one coarse mip node you read fillFraction→OPACITY + averaged emission→LIGHT (light-tree) + temperature→SPECTRAL COLOR redshifted by that same node's mip-level distance → a distant galaxy renders as translucent, glowing, redshifted fog IN ONE SAMPLE, no special-case code. Occupancy, radiance, distance, spectrum are all the same mip-averaging read at the LOD solid-angle chose. Also a natural residency story ([[lazy-procedural-delta-baseline-program]] / [[tiered-esvo-observer-address-direction]]): far tiers never materialize fine voxels — their coarse fill-fraction IS the correct appearance. **This is not a limitation, it's the whole model working as designed** — see the two elements below (seed-based lazy baseline; mip-map IS the far-view mechanism). BOUNDARIES: (a) scalar fill-fraction is ISOTROPIC coverage — can't distinguish a thin flat disc (ring edge-on vs face-on) from a diffuse cloud at the same fill %; anisotropic 6-direction mips recover directional coverage (why they exist), full sub-voxel arrangement is gone — fine for LOOK, want anisotropic for correct ring-thinness-by-angle. (b) This is alpha-compositing / emission-ABSORPTION, NOT multiple scattering — gives translucency + glow, NOT phase functions / god-rays / multi-scatter (that's the heavier participating-media layer below, a different cost — don't conflate). (c) Visual/phenomenological gate.
- **Seed-based lazy baseline makes "you can only see what's materialized" a non-issue at scale (user 2026-07-10).** The concern "a telescope/coarse view can only resolve detail that was actually stored" evaporates once the sim baseline is IMPLICITLY seed-based + lazily generated: the coarse view is rendered from the *rule* (the seed + procedural recipe), not from materialized voxel data. You don't load-and-materialize the fine structure to draw a distant planet — you evaluate the baseline rule shallowly (to whatever depth the LOD asked for) and render THAT. At planetary/stellar/galactic scale the per-object DELTAS (edits away from the procedural baseline) are individually sub-pixel and imperceptible, so a low-res rule-generated view is not just acceptable, it's *indistinguishable* from the "true" thing at that distance. **The inversion that matters:** materializing full-resolution data at these scales is the thing that CANNOT work (nothing would function — memory/bandwidth blow up instantly); the seed-based coarse render is the ONLY thing that functions, and it's correct-looking by construction. So "render a coarse view of lazy rule-based materialization" is the ordinary, expected path — not a special or degraded mode. Composes directly: coarse mip node's fill-fraction/emission/temperature (the reads above) are computed from the baseline RULE at that level, deltas layered in only where materialized (near / edited). Ties to [[lazy-procedural-delta-baseline-program]] (recipe-first rendering; deltas are the cache) + [[tiered-esvo-observer-address-direction]] (far tiers procedural-only).
- **The mip-map IS the far-view mechanism — this is what it was always for (user 2026-07-10).** Reading a coarse mip to draw a distant blurry planet/ring/galaxy isn't a workaround grafted onto the renderer; it is the mip pyramid performing its literal purpose — a level-of-detail read of a hierarchy, where the coarse level is a legitimate, precomputed (or rule-evaluated) summary of everything below it. The telescope/FOV-zoom (bullet below) and the "distant solid looks volumetric" pillar (above) are both just *consequences* of the mip existing: narrow the FOV → the solid-angle LOD selects a finer level → more of the baseline rule is evaluated → the blurry shape sharpens. There is no separate "distant object renderer" to build; the far view is the same traversal reading a coarser (rule-or-data) level. Don't over-engineer this — the correct appearance of a far object is *already* the coarse-mip read, and the seed-based baseline supplies that read whether or not anything was ever materialized there.
- **Atmospheric color from composition = per-medium spectral FILTER (a multiply, not a shift).** A gas mixture absorbs/scatters specific wavelength BANDS (CO2/methane/Rayleigh λ^-4/sodium-D…): transmitted = source spectrum × transmission(λ), applied once per medium-SEGMENT, not per sample. RGB can only do a 3-channel tint that muddies every gas's distinct bands into one multiply → "methane vs CO2" atmosphere color needs banded absorption in spectral space. This finally MOTIVATES participating media in the voxel traversal (the voxel world is well-suited to it — a medium is a voxel property along the ray segment).
- **Telescope / FOV-zoom is built-in from the FOV-driven mip traversal (user 2026-07-10).** VIXEN's LOD already selects a mip by the SOLID ANGLE a voxel subtends on screen (≈ `voxelWorldSize / distance`, projected through FOV onto the pixel grid). FOV enters that formula directly — so zooming a telescope IS narrowing the FOV, which shrinks solid-angle-per-pixel, which makes the same distant object subtend more pixels, which makes the exact same LOD math select a FINER mip. No telescope feature to build: turn the FOV knob and distant things resolve in more detail as a consequence. What falls out for free: (1) narrow FOV → a distant planet that was one coarse translucent blob resolves toward a blurry-then-sharper shape (rings emerge from "fuzzy disc"); (2) the volumetric-opacity transition rides along — coarse mip → low fill-fraction → translucent blob; finer mip on zoom → higher fill-fraction → firms toward solid, so "blurry translucent → concrete planet/rings as you zoom" is the SAME continuous mip transition driven by FOV. CAVEATS, both resolved by the two elements above: (a) you can only resolve detail that EXISTS in the tree — but with the seed-based lazy baseline, "exists" means "the rule can be evaluated deeper," not "was materialized," so zoom drives deeper RULE evaluation (procedural detail is effectively unbounded; only stored-delta detail is bounded by what was baked); (b) elegant coupling — aiming a telescope (narrowing FOV onto a target) IS a residency/materialization request: "I'm looking closely here, evaluate/stream this deeper." The FOV-narrowing that drives finer LOD is the same signal that drives deeper materialization. Ties to [[lazy-procedural-delta-baseline-program]] (recipe-vs-materialized depth) + [[tiered-esvo-observer-address-direction]].
- **Spectrum representation fork (the one real efficiency decision):** PARAMETRIC (store temperature for blackbody sources + a few absorption-band coefficients for media) is the efficient winner for the target use cases (stars+Doppler, atmospheres+composition): redshift = ×temperature (one float), atmosphere = ×few bands. Fall back to a BINNED spectrum (8–16 samples) only for arbitrary non-blackbody emitters or arbitrary measured gas spectra (redshift = resample bins, absorption = per-bin multiply — general but a small vector per emitter/photon).

## 3. Open questions (empirical/perceptual — for a research pass + prototype, not first-principles design)
- **Photon backbone vs ReSTIR+DDGI is a REPLACEMENT fork, not additive** (user 2026-07-10): if the photon layer hits budget it SUBSUMES direct+indirect+emission in one mechanism — no reason to also run a separate ReSTIR/analytic direct pass (double-counting). Decision gated on the M5 per-ray cost measurement + a photon prototype's budget, NOT committed now. Inc1's foundation (TraceWorld seam, HitRecord split, TraceWorldShadow visibility primitive, ShadowConfig) is BACKBONE-AGNOSTIC — it serves whichever backbone wins, so building it now is not wasted. NOTE the shadow RAY (visibility primitive) is NOT the light PASS (integrator): photon mapping still needs visibility tracing (its light-trace pass IS occlusion testing), so the seam survives; only the small direct-integrator shader would be replaced.
- Do probe cells store accumulated spectra or waveforms (the FFT-or-not choice above)?
- Does the emission mip-cut + ReSTIR reservoir compose or double-count (mip node vs its children as candidates)?
- Do mip-AVERAGED photons retain enough spectral variety, or does averaging wash the spectrum toward grey? (averaging spectra ≠ averaging RGB.)
- What distance kernel (how amplitude and phase vary with miss distance) actually reads as a WAVE rather than mush? Tune-and-look.
- How/where does spectral→RGB conversion happen at the final gather, and how many spectral samples per photon are enough?
- Do mip-aggregated (averaged) photons retain enough spectral variety to matter, or does averaging wash the spectrum toward grey? (Averaging spectra ≠ averaging RGB — needs checking.)
- Does the emission mip-cut + ReSTIR reservoir compose, or double-count emitters? (mip node vs its children as light candidates.)
- Recommended: run the research harness (spectral real-time rendering; blackbody/temperature-driven emission; spectral photon mapping; distance-kernel/kernel-based wave approximations; spectral↔RGB in a voxel pipeline) BEFORE committing an increment — same grounding discipline as the base lighting program.
